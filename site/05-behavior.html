Behavior notes with AI

Pets can't tell us how they feel in words. Their behavior is the message. AI helps you turn casual observations into organized notes your vet can actually use.

Step 1: Keep a running behavior log

Jot down what you see, when, and what happened right before. You don't need full sentences:

  • Date and time
  • What the pet did (or didn't do)
  • What was happening at the time
  • How long it lasted

Template: templates/behavior-log.csv

Step 2: Ask AI to spot patterns

After a week or two, paste your log into an AI tool — using general descriptions only, no names or addresses.

"Here are behavior notes for my cat over two weeks: [paste log, no names or addresses]. Help me organize them into: things that happen regularly, things that changed recently, and anything that might be worth asking a vet about. Use plain language."

Read the AI's output. Your observations come first.

Step 3: Prepare for vet conversations

"Turn these behavior observations into a short, bulleted list I can show my vet. Keep it under one page and use plain language."

Print it and bring it to the appointment.

What to watch for (general examples)

  • Changes in eating or drinking
  • Changes in sleep or energy
  • Hiding more than usual
  • New sounds (whining, meowing, howling)
  • Accidents in the house
  • Reluctance to jump, climb, or use stairs

None of these mean a specific problem on their own. They're reasons to call your vet and describe what you've seen.

Safety checks

  • Behavior notes support — never replace — a vet's exam
  • Never put your address, vet's name, or client information into a public AI tool
  • If your pet seems in pain or distressed, call your vet right away
